# Non-affiliation disclaimer wording

## Question

How can the README identify this as independent research inspired by IndyDevDan's public demonstration without implying endorsement or claiming immunity from intellectual-property liability?

## Findings

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes unprotected ideas, methods and systems from protected original expression. This is not a determination that every aspect of this implementation or its presentation is unprotected. [Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)

Trademark infringement can turn on likely confusion about source or sponsorship. The README therefore expressly disclaims affiliation, sponsorship and endorsement and limits references to identification and attribution. This wording is not a legal clearance or a waiver of another party's rights. [USPTO guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/page/about-trademark-infringement)

## Wording decisions

- Identify the project as an independent research experiment pursuing the results shown in the linked video.
- Acknowledge the intentional inspiration; do not claim that every similarity is accidental, indirect or legally irrelevant.
- Preserve third-party rights and distinguish project-authored MIT code from separately licensed material.
- Make no claim of blanket fair use, non-infringement, or immunity from liability.

Pros: makes the relationship clear and preserves accurate attribution. Cons: text alone cannot establish rights to reused material or decide infringement. Assumptions: no affiliation or endorsement has been granted; these are U.S. sources, not a multi-jurisdiction IP clearance. Open question: a definitive legal assessment of particular protected expression or marks would require review of the actual materials and applicable law.

## Name and appearance follow-up

Recommendation: choose a distinct name after clearance and create an independent interface composition while retaining functional requirements. This is risk reduction, not a finding of infringement or a guarantee against claims. The initial reconstruction intentionally studied the demo, so do not describe the resemblance as accidental.

The USPTO recommends searching federal records and common-law use: [comprehensive clearance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/comprehensive-clearance-search-similar-trademarks). Copyright can cover copyrightable screen displays as well as code: [Circular 61](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ61.pdf). Product-design trade dress presents a separate question involving functionality and acquired distinctiveness: [TMEP guidance](https://tmep.uspto.gov/RDMS/TMEP/print?href=TMEP-1200d1e835.html&version=current). No determination of IndyDevDan's specific trademark, trade-dress, patent or copyright rights has been made.

Pros: independent branding and visual design can reduce confusion and avoid unnecessary copying. Cons: cosmetic changes cannot cure unauthorized code or asset reuse and may not sufficiently distinguish protected expression; an attorney must assess specific rights and similarity for meaningful clearance. Keep dependency licenses, source attribution, and truthful development records.
